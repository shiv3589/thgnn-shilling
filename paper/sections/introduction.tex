\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

As recommender systems become critical infrastructure for e-commerce and content platforms, the sophistication of shilling attacks has escalated dramatically. Early heuristic attacks — injecting users with uniform high ratings on target items — are now trivially detected by statistical anomaly scoring. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) subsequently raised the bar by producing profiles that mimic real user rating distributions. Most recently, LLM-agent frameworks such as Agent4SR can generate entire user personas with coherent review histories, interaction patterns, and rating sequences indistinguishable from genuine users. This escalating arms race raises a fundamental empirical question: which attack families genuinely defeat which detection paradigms, and why?

We identify a critical gap in the literature: no systematic study has evaluated the full spectrum from heuristic to LLM-agent attacks against both feature-based and graph-based detectors under a unified experimental protocol. Existing work evaluates detectors against a narrow slice of attack types, making it impossible to characterise how detection difficulty scales with attack sophistication.

TH-GNN is a unified architecture built on three non-redundant signal streams,
designed to probe which of these streams remains informative as attack
sophistication increases.
The structural backbone is a two-layer Heterogeneous Graph Transformer that
represents users, reviews, and items as distinct node types connected by typed
\texttt{writes}, \texttt{about}, and \texttt{rates} edges, learning per-type and
per-relation attention patterns that capture the qualitatively different
fingerprints each node type carries.
Timestamp information is woven directly into every edge's attention logit via a
learnable sinusoidal encoding, letting the model recognise compressed inter-arrival
times that betray coordinated injection without requiring explicit campaign-level
supervision.
After the graph encoder, a cross-modal attention block fuses the structural
embedding with mean-pooled RoBERTa representations of the user's reviews and the
items they rated, integrating the textual and relational views under a single
learned attention distribution.
A lightweight GRU over each user's log-inter-arrival sequence provides an explicit
burstiness signal orthogonal to both graph topology and text content.
A two-layer MLP over the concatenated streams produces the final fake-user score.

Used this way, TH-GNN's results are revealing rather than uniformly favourable:
it maintains detection rate (DR) above 0.99 across every attack family we
study, but its false alarm rate rises sharply on both heuristic and
LLM-agent attacks, so its F\textsubscript{1} trails feature-based baselines
on three of the four families. The one attack where TH-GNN is competitive is
AUSH, the GAN-based attack that instead defeats the feature-based baselines —
a pattern we analyse in detail in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item We propose \textbf{TH-GNN}, a Temporal Heterogeneous Graph Neural Network combining heterogeneous graph attention, relative temporal encoding, and multimodal review-text fusion — to our knowledge, the first detector to jointly model all three signal types for shilling detection.
  \item We conduct the \textbf{first systematic empirical study} evaluating four attack families of increasing sophistication (heuristic, GAN-based, LLM-agent) against both feature-based and graph-based detectors under a unified protocol.
  \item We reveal a \textbf{critical asymmetry}: feature-based detectors collapse against GAN-based AUSH profiles (Anti-FakeU F1 drops from 0.937 to 0.313) while maintaining high recall, whereas TH-GNN maintains consistent DR\,$>$\,0.99 across all attacks but suffers precision degradation — suggesting the two paradigms capture orthogonal anomaly signals.
  \item We reveal that \textbf{feature-based detectors remain effective against the current Agent4SR implementation} (best F1\,=\,0.862), primarily because LLM-generated profiles still exhibit detectable volume and temporal-concentration artifacts under the experimental setup (50-filler cap, injection-window clustering); graph-based TH-GNN suffers precision collapse under the resulting distribution shift (F1\,=\,0.035). The open frontier is detection when these incidental artifacts are removed and when graph-based detectors can be calibrated without label access.
  \item We release a \textbf{reproducible benchmark} covering 33 experimental configurations across 4 attack families, with code at \url{https://github.com/shiv3589/thgnn-shilling}.
\end{itemize}
